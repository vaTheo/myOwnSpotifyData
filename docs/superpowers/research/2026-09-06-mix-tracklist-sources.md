# Getting the tracklist of a SoundCloud DJ mix from a link (2026-09-06)

Output of a research workflow (6 researchers, 5 fact-checkers with live probes, 1 synthesis). Scope: what it would actually take for the owner to paste a SoundCloud link to an hour-plus DJ mix into the app and get back every track, in order, with the time it starts — assessed for a browser-only static Preact app on GitHub Pages (no server, no secrets), with the owner's underground house and techno in mind.

Everything below was probed on **2026-09-06** from France with curl and a real Chromium, and cross-checked by a second reviewer with independent probes; refuted claims are dropped or corrected in place, unverifiable ones carry **(unverified)**. **One caveat on provenance:** five of the six research areas were fact-checked; the sixth (open-source *projects* — the GitHub survey and the academic literature, `oss-projects.md`) had no second pass, so its figures are single-source and are marked *(single-source)* where they carry weight.

Raw probe output is re-readable under `raw/`: `raw/audio-access/`, `raw/recognition-apis/`, `raw/open-source/`, `raw/oss-projects/`, `raw/community/`, `raw/architecture/` (first pass), and `raw/check-audio-access/`, `raw/check-recognition-apis/`, `raw/check-open-source/`, `raw/check-community/`, `raw/check-architecture/` (verification pass).

Reference material for a "paste a mix link" feature; the design spec, once written, is the authority.

## 1. The short answer

**A link alone cannot do what was asked.** Not because of one blocker but because of two independent ones, each verified today. First, a static page on `https://vatheo.github.io` can never reach the audio: `api-v2.soundcloud.com`, the endpoint that hands out the signed stream URL, runs an origin allowlist. Control test on the same 401 — `Origin: https://soundcloud.com` → `access-control-allow-origin: https://soundcloud.com`; `Origin: https://vatheo.github.io` → **no `access-control-*` header at all**, and the `OPTIONS` preflight returns 200 with no CORS headers either, so the browser refuses before it sends. (The CDN *behind* it is wide open — `cf-media.sndcdn.com` answers `access-control-allow-origin: *` with `Range` support, and the HLS playlist is 702 signed 10-second segments, also `*` — so the wall is the resolver, not the audio.) Second, even with the audio in hand, SoundCloud's [API Terms of Use](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/terms-of-use) forbid the analysis by purpose: User Content may not be used "to create fingerprints or any other identifying digital files of User Content", the app "must not include file-save functionality, or otherwise designed to cache, download or persistently store any User Content", and "You must not use the SoundCloud API for any so-called stream ripping, stream capture or similar applications". That clause follows the credentials onto any machine, so **a server component does not fix it** — which is worth saying plainly, because the owner has never been asked about a server and this is the one area where the answer would be "it changes nothing".

**What a link alone *does* buy, free and from the phone.** Two keyless lookups, both verified CORS-open from our exact origin today. **TrackId.net** — `GET https://trackid.net/api/public/audiostreams?url=<percent-encoded permalink>` returns `access-control-allow-origin: https://vatheo.github.io`, no key, no account, against a corpus of **383,931** already-analysed mixes; when a mix is in it the response *is* the deliverable (`startTime`, `endTime`, `artist`, `title`, `label`, and `referenceCount` = how many other mixes contain that track). Its measured yield on the one real underground mix probed is sobering and is *their* number: **18 identified spans covering 62.2 of 190.6 minutes — 32.6% of the runtime**. And **SoundCloud oEmbed** — `GET https://soundcloud.com/oembed?format=json&url=…` returns `access-control-allow-origin: *` and the uploader's full, untruncated `description`, which sometimes contains a tracklist the uploader wrote by hand, timestamps and all. How often is the question that decides the feature, and the honest answer is a range with its frames named: **56/311 = 18.0%** on a search-derived sample (upward-biased — one of the eight search queries was literally `tracklist`), **6/500 = 1.2%** across every Dekmantel podcast upload, **1/13** on a MixesDB-catalogued sample, and **0/40** on a re-measurement through oEmbed itself over randomly drawn ≥30-minute mixes from two real DJ-mix channels. The phrase "one mix in five" must not survive into a spec. Whether a description carries a tracklist turns out to be a property of the *uploader*, not the mix: five of six sampled accounts were 0 for 159, and every hit came from one account at 4 of 13.

**What it costs to go further, and how complete it gets.** Recognition is buyable and it is not expensive: AudD's enterprise endpoint chunks an hours-long file server-side and returns every hit in one request, at $5/1000 requests and one request per 12 s of audio — **$0.38 for a 1-hour mix at a 48-second stride, $1.50 at full 12-second resolution** ([audd.io](https://audd.io/) `PRICING`; [docs.audd.io/enterprise.md](https://docs.audd.io/enterprise.md)). But it cannot be driven from the app (the token is spendable and the bundle is public), it cannot be pointed at a SoundCloud URL without a paid plan (`error 904`: "Music Recognition by URL on our enterprise endpoint is only available to paid users"), and its recovered terms of service carry a clause aimed squarely at this app's design: **"Without permission from AudD, our customers shall not cache the data they received from the Service in order to re-use it instead of making similar or the same request to the Service"** ([Wayback `20210411214607`](https://web.archive.org/web/20210411214607id_/https://audd.io/terms/); the live page has been a broken Docusaurus shell since mid-2021). That is the same clause shape that killed Discogs in the 2026-09-05 artist-reach research, and it must be resolved with AudD before anything AudD-derived is persisted. On completeness: the one published measurement of this exact problem — Sonnleitner, Arzt & Widmer, ISMIR 2016, on 7 h 16 of real discotheque mixes **with every played track already in the reference database** — reports per-second accuracy of **0.419 (Audfprint) / 0.247 (Panako) / 0.741 (Qfp)** and notes that two of the three "claim a wrong track in around 50% of the cases where the correct track should be identifiable" *(single-source)*. Add unreleased promos and dubplates, which are in no fingerprint database at all, and the realistic deliverable is **a draft tracklist with explicit `ID · unidentified · 12:34 – 17:02` gaps**, not the full tracklist.

**So the shape that survives all of this**: paste a link → free TrackId.net lookup → free oEmbed description parse → if neither answers, say so plainly and offer a **paste-your-own-tracklist box** as a first-class element (MixesDB carried a tracklist for **23 of 24** sampled mixes, 14 of them with `[min]` offsets, and the app parses pasted text with the same code) plus the embedded SoundCloud player with `show_comments=true` beside it, because on 2 of 9 mixes censused a single comment holds a whole pasted tracklist — on Four Tet's three-hour Loop set, a complete one in `hh:mm:ss`. Audio recognition, if the owner wants it, belongs on the laptop against a file they lawfully hold, exported to the phone like the Rekordbox XML already is — and it is gated on two owner decisions, not on any missing technology.

**The oEmbed details a spec will need.** The response has exactly 12 keys (`author_name`, `author_url`, `description`, `height`, `html`, `provider_name`, `provider_url`, `thumbnail_url`, `title`, `type`, `version`, `width`) — **no `duration` field**; the numeric track id is recoverable from the `html` iframe `src`. It **does not truncate**: across the 7 longest descriptions sampled, the oEmbed field is byte-identical to the api-v2 field (3998=3998, 3960=3960, 3945=3945, 3829=3829, 3636=3636, 3494=3494); the longest across 393 tracks is 3,998 chars with nothing over 4,000, consistent with a ~4,000-char cap **(unverified)**. Descriptions contain raw HTML fragments and `\r\n`, so entities must be unescaped and tags stripped before line matching. **The timestamp grammar must be written out explicitly** — optional `[` or `(`, one or two `H:` groups, `.` as well as `:` as separator, optional closing bracket, optional `-` / `–` / `.` / `)` before the artist. A bracket-only `^\[\d{1,3}:\d{2}\]` matcher silently drops **everything after the first hour of every long mix** (`[1:00:30]` fails it), and **34 of 311** mixes in the sample carry ≥4 such `H:MM:SS` lines. The showcase mix `soundcloud.com/scatolo/fantasm-boiler-gotec-club-2-h` has **51** tracklist lines running to `[2:10:50]`, not the 23 a narrow regex counts.

**The TrackId.net guard, and why it is mandatory.** `?url=` genuinely filters and is an **exact** match: the exact permalink returns `rowCount: 1` and that record; a truncated prefix, a trailing slash, an upper-cased path and the bare host all return `rowCount: 0`. But **any unrecognised query parameter is silently ignored** — `?searchTerm=`, `?slug=`, `?permalink=` *and a control `?zzz=1`* all return HTTP 200 with `rowCount: 383931` and the same unfiltered first page. This is ordinary REST behaviour, not three specific traps, and it means a client that trusted a 200 would show a random YouTube mix as the answer. **Accept only when `rowCount === 1` and the returned `url` equals the pasted URL.** Never derive the slug from the permalink (their slug is title-derived; the literal last permalink segment 404s), and never trust `trackCount` — the listing said 16, the detail record said 0, the detection processes held 18 rows. The corpus is not constant either: 383,928 → 383,931 in one day.

**The single blocking unknown, for both paid vendors: can either server fetch a SoundCloud URL?** AudD names SoundCloud only in **marketing FAQ copy** on its homepage — its documentation never mentions SoundCloud, and its streams doc enumerates only Twitch and YouTube as natively supported. ACRCloud's platforms list is `"Youtube,Twitter, Tiktok, Vimeo, Brightcove...etc"` — self-declared partial, so the omission is not evidence of absence; the single occurrence of "soundcloud" across all 77 fetched ACRCloud docs pages is about *outbound* metadata linking, not ingestion. Neither can be tested without paying (AudD gates URL input behind `error 904`). **On current evidence neither vendor is established for "paste a SoundCloud link".** This is a blocking unknown for both, not a discriminator between them.

## 4. Open source, the literature, and the human tracklists — with their coverage limits

### 4.1 Open-source fingerprinting: no free engine both accepts mid-track excerpts and runs in a browser

| Option | Status | The measurement that decides it |
|---|---|---|
| **AcoustID lookup** (`api.acoustid.org/v2/lookup`) | **No** | Two structural reasons, both measured. (1) Candidates are filtered on declared **`duration` within ±7 s** (`FINGERPRINT_MAX_LENGTH_DIFF = 7`; reproduced exactly — alive at −7…+7, dead at ±8), so you must already know a track's full length to look up a chunk of it. (2) Stored fingerprints cover only about the **first 117 s** of a track while the stored `length` is the whole track: **802 of 802** sampled records in AcoustID's own published export exceed 130 s of declared length, median fingerprint **948 items = 117.4 s**, none over 1,000 items. For a six-minute club track roughly two thirds of the timeline is absent from any given stored fingerprint. Excerpt matching measured 0/14 with an honest duration, 3/14 with an oracle duration — and those 3 only because their previews start at the track's beginning. **Two commonly-cited reasons are refuted and should not be repeated:** the ±9.90 s alignment window (`TRACK_MAX_OFFSET = 80`) is real but not a general barrier — 3 of 5 tracks returned the *correct* MusicBrainz recording at query offsets of 15–22 s; and the "57 minutes of lookups" figure is wrong by 20× because `/v2/lookup` accepts an undocumented `batch=1` form carrying ~20 fingerprints per request (HTTP 413 at 25) |
| **AcoustID `/v2/track/list_by_mbid`** | **Keep, for other features** | **No API key at all**, `access-control-allow-origin: *` from our origin, batched. Useless for mix ID; the only keyless, CORS-open, terms-friction-free endpoint in this area (216/285 recordings known) |
| **Chromaprint / fpcalc** (incl. WASM) | **Secondary, with a warning** | Extraction is cheap and streams: **2.39 s / 6.4 MB peak RSS** for a whole 1-hour MP3 natively, 4.77 s for 120 timestamped 30-s chunks; `rusty-chromaprint-wasm` 0.1.5 (MIT) exists and the app is a Vite build, so bundling is not the problem. **The pitch fader is.** Correct-recording survival measured with `meta=recordings`: turntable-style speed+pitch **0% (2 of 3 tracks) to ±1%**; key-lock (tempo only) **0% to ±2%**. Worse than failing, it fails *confidently*: a speed-changed query returns wrong recordings at scores of **0.514–0.65**, and no threshold separates them from the right answers at 0.539 and 0.580 |
| **Olaf** (landmark, WASM target) | **No** — was the previous primary candidate, refuted by source read | The `make web` target compiles `src/olaf_db_mem.c`, whose own comment reads "Only a single song is currently supported for the memory store"; its `olaf_db_new()` does `(void)(db_file_folder);` — discards the database folder — and points at a const array from a compiled-in header; `olaf_db_store()` voids all four arguments and does nothing. The browser build answers "is this **one** song in this mix", for a song baked in at compile time. The 100,000-track / 15 GB / 80×-realtime figure is the **desktop LMDB build** (README "Limitations") and must never be quoted beside the browser claim |
| **Panako** (the only speed-robust engine) | **Fallback, and the evidence is two-sided** | Its README states the DJ case exactly: "During a DJ-set speed changes are almost always present… a music search algorithm robust against pitch shifting, time stretching and speed changes is desired. The Panako algorithm allows such changes." It is the **only** configuration in this study that plausibly survives a real ±6% fader, and the laptop/full-length-library/export-a-match-file shape sidesteps the ±7 s duration oracle and the ~117 s ceiling in one move. **Against that:** the one published measurement on real DJ mixes puts **Panako v1.4, standard parameters, untuned, last of three** (§4.2). AGPL-3.0 (propagates to the whole app), JVM + LMDB, last push 2024-05-31 |
| **Deezer 30-s previews as the reference corpus** | **Secondary, with a geometry problem** | Solves "where does reference audio come from" without the owner uploading anything: `api.deezer.com/track/isrc:{ISRC}` → preview, CDN verified `access-control-allow-origin: *`, `access-control-allow-headers: Range`, `audio/mpeg`. Measured: **119/139 (85.6%)** ISRCs resolve, **109/139 (78.4%)** have a preview, underground subset 71/87 (81.6%), duration agreement within 7 s for 118/119. But only **139 of 285 (48.8%)** MusicBrainz recordings carry an ISRC at all, and a 30-second window is **7–10%** of a 5–7 minute track — it only matches if the DJ played that part. Preview offsets are **not fixed**: 3 of 14 start within 9.9 s of the track start, one ~30–45 s in, two could not be located in the first 120 s |
| **Self-hosting the AcoustID database** | **No** | Published daily as incremental JSONL; one day's file is 136,926,711 bytes gzipped, archive runs monthly from 2011-08, CC BY-SA 3.0. Hundreds of GB for a browser-only app, and the corpus carries the same ~117 s-per-fingerprint ceiling. Also 41 days stale against a "daily" promise (newest directory `2026/2026-07/`) |
| **dejavu / audfprint / Echoprint** | **No** | `worldveil/dejavu` Python+MySQL 6,786★ pushed 2024-04-22; `dpwe/audfprint` 611★ pushed 2019-09-24; `spotify/echoprint-codegen` 962★ **archived**, its server long gone. All would need both a port and a reference database |

**The free OSS *tools* that do work are all clients of somebody else's database** *(single-source, `oss-projects.md`)*. `betmoar/tracklistify` (MIT, 71★, pushed 2026-09-03, ~723 tests: 60 s window / 50 s stride → 72 calls/hour, confidence gate, time-window dedup, JSON output), `skip5this/mix-id` (`npx mix-id`, 18 s @ 30 s → 120 calls/hour), `b1scoito/ripthatset` (12 s contiguous → 300 calls/hour, clusters with `min-matches 2`, `max-gap 3`, and emits `ID - ID` for gaps ≥30 s), `chefkjd/MixSplitR` (desktop GUI, 86★). Every one of them calls Shazam's undocumented endpoint by default, or the owner's own ACRCloud key. Three design ideas worth stealing regardless of route: **cluster-and-vote merging with an explicit `ID` row for unfilled runs** (`ripthatset`), an **adaptive second pass at half the stride only where the first pass found nothing** (`hey-dj`), and reading Shazam's `frequency_skew`/`time_skew` as *both* a confidence signal and evidence the DJ pitched the record. Do **not** copy `CAPJackie/soundcloud-setlist-extractor`: it scrapes the `client_id`, resolves the signed HLS stream and fingerprints it — a point-by-point enumeration of what the API Terms forbid.

### 4.2 The one academic measurement of this exact problem

**Sonnleitner, Arzt & Widmer, "Landmark-Based Audio Fingerprinting for DJ Mix Monitoring", ISMIR 2016** (CC BY 4.0, [zenodo.org/records/1417008](https://zenodo.org/records/1417008)) *(single-source)*. Two datasets — 8 mixes recorded off the mixing desk in real discotheques (7 h 16, 296 reference tracks) and 10 CC-licensed Mixotic netlabel mixes (11 h 23, techno / Chicago house / deep-tech / dub-techno — the owner's world). Mixes cut into non-overlapping 20-second queries, scored per second, no temporal smoothing, three systems as published and untuned:

| Set | Audfprint | Panako v1.4 | Qfp |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Disco — accuracy | 0.419 | **0.247** | 0.741 |
| Disco — precision | 0.513 | **0.452** | 0.917 |
| Mixotic — accuracy | 0.637 | **0.360** | 0.876 |
| Mixotic — precision | 0.680 | **0.432** | 0.959 |

In the authors' words: *"just between 25% and 74% of detectable seconds were assigned to the correct reference track… Audfprint and Panako claim a wrong track in around 50% of the cases where the correct track should be identifiable."* Three things follow. **This is a ceiling, measured with the answers already in the database** — every played track's reference was indexed, so real-world coverage can only be worse. **The winner is unobtainable** — Qfp's only public implementation (`mbortnyck/qfp`, 42★) has no LICENSE file and no commit since 2018-04-13. And the claim about Panako must be stated narrowly: *v1.4, standard parameters, untuned, in 2016*. The algorithm has moved since and no measurement of current Panako on DJ mixes exists — so the honest position is that Panako's README is right about the problem and the one measurement that exists is stale and unflattering, not that Panako is bad.

The adjacent literature does not help: **Kim et al., ISMIR 2020** ("unmixing", 1,557 mixes) aligns a mix against originals you already hold, *given a tracklist you already have*. It is a different problem wearing the same words.

### 4.4 What the parser must handle

All six row shapes are evidenced in the corpora, and a spec that assumes three will drop real tracklists:

1. `1. Artist - Title` — numbered (78-row example).
2. `Artist - Title` — bare dash, often under a literal `Tracklist:` header (4 of 6 hit descriptions had one).
3. `a1. Artist - Title` — side-position prefixes.
4. `[0:00]: Artist - Title` / `[1:00:30] Artist - Title` — bracketed offsets, **one or two `H:` groups**.
5. `00:00 Artist - Title` / `hh:mm:ss Artist – Title` — bare offsets, seen in one description and in the bulk-paste comments.
6. `[42] Artist - Track [Label]` — the MixesDB house style, which is what the owner will actually paste.

Constraints, corrected against the data: all three dash characters (hyphen-only in the sampled descriptions, but **en-dash throughout the Four Tet comment**), `.` as well as `:` as a time separator, `ID` / `?` / `unknown` as legitimate values, `[Label]` brackets **rare in descriptions (1 of 6) and common on MixesDB**, and a run-of-≥5 matching lines before accepting a block, to keep prose out. Some descriptions link out instead of listing ("Full tracklist here: dkmn.tl/270-ErisDrew", "find the track list on thelotradio.com") — detect that and show the link rather than an empty state.

## 5. Four end-to-end architectures

### 5.1 Option A — pure browser (static app only, no server)

**Verdict: cannot be built, and the reason is one gate earlier than expected.** The blocker is not "a recognition API with a publishable key" (though neither AudD's spendable `api_token` nor ACRCloud's `access_secret` is publishable) — it is that **the page can never hold the audio to send them**, per §2 row 10. What A *can* do is everything in §4.3's "yes" rows: the TrackId.net lookup, the oEmbed description parse, the embedded player, and the paste-in box. That is a real feature and it is the recommendation; it is just not the feature that was asked for.

- **Setup:** none beyond the app itself. No account, no key, no second setting — CLAUDE.md's "Only one setting exists: `VITE_SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID`" stays true.
- **Cost per 1-hour mix:** **$0**, two HTTP requests, under a second.
- **Completeness:** a *complete* tracklist only from the description parse or the paste-in box (a description carries one on 0–18% of mixes, frame-dependent; a paste always does). **A TrackId.net hit is not a complete tracklist** — its one measured example resolved 18 spans covering 32.6% of the runtime, so it arrives as a draft with `ID` gaps like any recognition result. Nothing at all otherwise, unless the owner pastes.
- **Terms risk:** lowest available. oEmbed is advertised by SoundCloud in every track page's `<head>`; TrackId.net publishes no terms of use at all, which is itself the objection.

### 5.2 Option B — static app + a tiny Cloudflare Worker holding the key

The browser POSTs the SoundCloud URL to a `*.workers.dev` endpoint; the Worker attaches the AudD token, forwards to `https://enterprise.audd.io/` with `url=`, and streams the JSON back. Audio never enters the Worker — which is what keeps it free: [Cloudflare's limits page](https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/limits/) states "**Waiting on network requests (such as `fetch()` calls…) does not count toward CPU time**" and "**There is no hard limit on duration for HTTP-triggered Workers**", so a pass-through Worker uses single-digit milliseconds against the free plan's 10 ms CPU limit, inside 100,000 requests/day, at **$0/month**. A Worker that decoded or segmented audio would blow that instantly and need the $5/month plan.

- **Setup:** a Cloudflare account; `wrangler` in `devDependencies`; ~30 lines of `worker.ts` plus `wrangler.toml`; `wrangler secret put AUDD_TOKEN`; **a second app setting** (`VITE_MIX_PROXY_URL`) in `.env` and CI; a second deploy target; and an authorisation gate, because a public `workers.dev` URL forwarding to a metered API is a spend hole and an `Origin` check is not a security control. (The clean gate for this repo: the app already holds a Spotify token, so the Worker can spend one subrequest on `/v1/me` and refuse unless the id is the owner's — a design proposal, not a probed claim.)
- **Cost per 1-hour mix:** **$0.38** at a 48-second stride, plus $0 infrastructure. **AudD's paid plan is required** — `url=` mode returns `error 904` for free users.
- **Time:** AudD's own example is `"execution_time": "38.335355495s"` for 63 chunks = 0.61 s/chunk, so ~46 s for 75 chunks — but the request is **synchronous and unresumable**, with no checkpoint, on a phone over mobile. That conflicts with the manual/resumable/checkpointed job pattern CLAUDE.md establishes for history import and playlist sync.
- **Terms risk:** the probed mix is `downloadable: false`, `license: all-rights-reserved`, so User Terms clause (i) is not satisfied — and instructing AudD to fetch does not move that obligation. Plus AudD's caching clause on the result. **Open: does `url=` even accept a SoundCloud *page* URL?** The docs say "a URL of a web page that contains audio or video", but `error 904` blocked the test. If it needs a direct media URL, the Worker has to move ~60 MB of audio and stops being free or simple.

### 5.3 Option C — owner-run script on their computer, result imported

```
yt-dlp -x --audio-format mp3 --audio-quality 5 <soundcloud url> -o mix.mp3
ffmpeg -i mix.mp3 -ac 1 -b:a 64k mix64.mp3          # ~29 MB for 1 h
curl https://enterprise.audd.io/ -F api_token="$AUDD_TOKEN" -F file=@mix64.mp3 \
     -F accurate_offsets=true -F skip=3 -F every=1 > mix.json
node tools/mix-to-json.mjs mix.json > mix-<slug>.json    # drop into #/import
```

- **Setup:** an AudD account (token stays on the laptop, never in the bundle); yt-dlp and ffmpeg as the only new binaries; Node 24 is already the toolchain, so the script is a `tools/` file. **The import path already exists** — `src/features/rekordbox.ts` + `rekordbox.worker.ts` + `rekordboxImport.ts` are the precedent: a dropped file, a worker, a `meta` summary, "Never rejects: every failure arrives through `onState`". A mix JSON is architecturally the same object.
- **Cost per 1-hour mix:** **$0.38** at `skip=3`, **$0** infrastructure. **Do not promise a free first mix:** "file POST is the free path" is an inference from `error 904`'s wording, and a bogus token on the enterprise endpoint returns `error 900` — the account "needs to have either a trial or an active subscription". Treat AudD as a paid account of unverified minimum.
- **What it does *not* give any more:** **ISRC is gated** — "You don't have to have an enterprise account in order to use the enterprise endpoint. But you need an enterprise account to get ISRCs and UPCs with the results… send an email to api@audd.io." Without that email, C joins the library through `src/model/match.ts` text matching, exactly the weakness TrackId.net is marked down for.
- **Terms risk:** highest of the four. It needs a copy of the mix, and for a typical `downloadable: false` set that is outside User Terms clause (i); plus AudD's caching clause on the persisted result.

### 5.4 Option D — Auto Shazam on the phone → Spotify playlist → the existing sync

From Apple's Shazam User Guide ([support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/shazam/deve025522b3/web](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/guide/shazam/deve025522b3/web)), verbatim: *"turn on **Sync Shazam Library to Spotify**. A 'My Shazam Tracks' playlist is created in Spotify, and your identified songs are automatically added to the playlist."* — with the caveat *"the apps must be installed on your device, and you must be a subscriber."*

- **Setup:** none in this app. `src/sync/runner.ts` already requests `items(added_at,…)`, `src/sync/items.ts` maps it, and `EntryRow.addedAt` is persisted, so **play order is reliable** and zero new code is needed.
- **Cost per 1-hour mix:** **$0** in money, **61 minutes of wall clock in real time**, with the mix played out loud into the phone's microphone.
- **Completeness:** best per-hit engine in the study, and exact Spotify track ids — the cleanest library join available anywhere in this research. The limit is a mechanism rather than a missing measurement: Shazam answers one identification at a time, so a layered transition yields one track or none, and unreleased promos are absent as everywhere.
- **Timing:** **(unverified)** — the Spotify sync is a background batch, so several identifications may share a near-identical `added_at`. Treat D as *an ordered tracklist without dependable start times* until measured. Note the web My Shazam library is gone (`shazam.com/myshazam` → 404 with `x-middleware-rewrite: /notfound`), so the Spotify playlist is not merely the nicest export path — it is the only one.
- **Terms risk:** **none identified.** Shazam listens to audio playing in the room; nothing is copied from SoundCloud.

### 5.5 The cost-and-time model, side by side (1-hour mix)

| | A — pure browser | B — app + Worker | C — laptop script + import | D — Auto Shazam → Spotify |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Money / mix | **$0** | $0.38 (AudD **paid plan required**) | $0.38 | **$0** |
| Infrastructure / month | $0 | $0 (Workers Free) | $0 | $0 |
| Owner setup | none | Cloudflare account, wrangler, 2nd deploy target, **2nd app setting**, auth gate | AudD account, yt-dlp + ffmpeg, one `tools/` script | Shazam app, Spotify subscription |
| Wall clock | < 1 s | ~46 s, synchronous and unresumable | ~1 min upload + ~46 s | **61 min, in real time** |
| Works from the phone alone | **yes** | yes | no — laptop, then sync | yes (the phone *is* the tool) |
| Output identifiers | artist/title/label (+ `referenceCount`) | artist/title/label, **ISRC only on request** | same | **Spotify track id** |
| Start times | yes, when the source has them | `offset − timecode` estimate | same | **(unverified)** — order yes |
| Completeness | complete from a description or a paste; ~⅓ of runtime + `ID` gaps from TrackId.net | ~⅓ of runtime, plus `ID` gaps | same | one answer per moment, so layered transitions yield one track or none |
| Terms risk | lowest (TrackId.net has no ToU) | download + AudD caching clause | download + AudD caching clause | **none identified** |

**Turning hits into a timeline** (whichever of B/C is used). The start estimate is `startEstimate = chunkOffset − timecode` (AudD) or `windowStart − play_offset_ms/1000` (ACRCloud) — the fields are now unambiguously documented: *"timecode is the position **in the original song**"*, *"offset is the position, in the audio file you submitted, of the start of the 12-second-long fragment"*. **Take the earliest hit in a run, not the median** — on AudD's own published example, mix time advances 48 s while song time advances 50 s, implying a playback rate of 1.02–1.06, so the estimate walks ~2 s later per 48 s of mix and a median is biased by run length. **Clamp at 0** — the same example yields −11 s and −33 s at `offset 00:00`. Do **not** build the timeline on `start_offset`/`end_offset`: AudD documents them as positions *inside the 12-second fragment*. Group runs on `isrc` **when present** and normalised artist+title otherwise (with ISRC behind an email request, text is the default path); tolerate a 1–2 window gap inside a run; require ≥2 hits to accept and mark single hits `possible`; let runs overlap, because a transition legitimately returns both records; and **emit every span ≥60 s with no accepted run as an explicit `ID · unidentified · 12:34 – 17:02` row.** A tracklist that silently omits the holes is a lie about the set.

## 6. Recommendation

**Build option A, and be honest in the UI about what it cannot do.** Layer 0 is two free keyless requests from the phone — TrackId.net `?url=` behind the `rowCount === 1` **and** URL-equality guard, then the oEmbed `description` parsed with the full six-shape, two-`H:`-group grammar of §4.4 — followed by a **paste-your-own-tracklist box as a first-class element, not an empty state**, and the embedded player with `show_comments=true` beside it. That combination is the only one in this research that costs nothing, needs no account, no key, no second setting and no server, works from the Android phone the owner actually uses, carries the lowest terms exposure of anything assessed, and still answers a real share of mixes completely. Every serious open-source tool in the survey independently put the text route ahead of the audio route, which is corroboration rather than coincidence. **Do not build audio recognition into the app**: the browser cannot get the audio (origin allowlist, verified by control test), the API Terms forbid fingerprinting User Content by purpose on any machine, and a spendable API key cannot live in a public bundle. **Do not promise the full tracklist**: with every played track already in the reference database the published accuracy for open fingerprinters on real discotheque mixes is 0.25–0.74 per second, the best commercial corpus reachable from a browser resolved 32.6% of one real underground mix's runtime, and unreleased dubs are in nobody's database — so the deliverable is a **draft with editable rows and explicit `ID` gaps**, which is cheaper to build than any accuracy improvement available and is what human tracklisters already write. If the owner wants more than that, the next step is **not** more research: it is one experiment (spend AudD's 300 free requests on a file POST of one hour-long mix whose tracklist the owner already knows, and count the hits) and two decisions — whether to seek AudD's written permission to cache results, and whether copying a mix the uploader did not mark downloadable is acceptable to them. Until both are settled, option C stays on the shelf, and **option D (Auto Shazam on the phone, into the Spotify playlist this app already syncs) is the zero-cost, zero-terms-risk baseline every paid option has to beat** — for the owner's own gigs it may simply be the answer.

## 7. Rejected, one line each

- **`api-v2.soundcloud.com` from the browser** — origin allowlist proved by control test (`soundcloud.com` echoed, our origin gets zero `access-control-*` headers) and the preflight returns 200 with no CORS headers, so the browser refuses before sending.
- **An extracted `client_id`** — the API Terms bar using "another person's client ID and/or Security Code", and the User Terms bar the web scraping that obtains it.
- **A registered SoundCloud app (Artist Pro + `client_secret`)** — a public bundle cannot hold a secret, Artist Pro is **annual-only at €83.88**, and the purpose clauses (fingerprints, AI input, stream capture, no persistent storage) would still forbid the analysis on any machine, server included.
- **Widget iframe + Web Audio** — `contentDocument` is `null` and `contentWindow.document` throws `SecurityError` in real Chromium; the `<audio>` element is in another origin's document, so there is nothing to fall back to.
- **`getDisplayMedia({audio:true})`** — MDN BCD says `false` for `chrome_android`, `firefox_android` and `safari_ios`, so it does not exist on the owner's device; on the laptop it exists and becomes the "stream capture" the User Terms prohibit.
- **`yt-dlp` against a `downloadable: false` mix** — User Terms (i) permits copying only where the uploader consented; the API ToU names the conduct "content piracy, stream ripping".
- **Mixcloud `sections`** — `[]` on 213/213 anonymous REST reads while the website serves populated `TrackSection` objects anonymously, so it is not a paywall but a dead field; and no `startSeconds` anywhere, so it would not give start times even if scraped. Do not buy a month to test it.
- **`app.mixcloud.com/graphql`** — OPTIONS returns 200 with `vary: origin` and zero `access-control-*` headers; browser-unusable.
- **1001Tracklists** — conceptually the best data that exists, with no API, zero CORS headers, an active Cloudflare Turnstile challenge, datacenter-IP blocking by their own FAQ, and a submission rule biased against the underground.
- **MixesDB as a dependency** — the best content in the study (23/24, 14/24 with offsets) on a wiki that 302'd every path to an upgrade notice on the day of this research; link out, do not depend.
- **SoundCloud timed comments** — unreachable three ways (api-v2 401 with zero ACAO, page HTML zero ACAO, `api-widget` pinned to `w.soundcloud.com`), and the census refutes the premise anyway: 15 ID-shaped comments in 971 against 142 unanswered requests.
- **SoundCloud podcast RSS** — hard-wired `access-control-allow-origin: https://www.radiofrance.fr` under three control conditions; superb as a research instrument, unusable from the app.
- **YouTube oEmbed** — CORS-clean and carries **no `description` field**, so the keyless route that works on SoundCloud is useless here.
- **Reddit `.json`** — 403 to an unregistered client (`old.reddit.com` 302s); blocked before content quality can be assessed.
- **Discogs** — already rejected by the 2026-09-05 artist-reach research on its six-hour freshness and no-caching clauses, and zero coverage of SoundCloud uploads by construction.
- **setlist.fm** — 403 without a registered key, and band-shaped rather than club-DJ-shaped.
- **LLM parsing of a description** — no key can live in a public Pages bundle, SoundCloud's ToU forbids copying User Content "as input to" AI services, and the dominant failure is that the text contains **no** tracklist, which no model fixes.
- **AcoustID lookup for mix chunks** — the ±7 s declared-duration filter means you must already know the track's length, and stored fingerprints cover only ~117 s (802/802 records, median 948 items), so mid-track excerpts fail in the ordinary case.
- **Olaf's WASM build** — holds exactly **one** reference song compiled into the binary (`(void)(db_file_folder);`, a no-op `olaf_db_store()`); adopting it means writing a new C storage backend, not compiling the existing web target.
- **Self-hosting the AcoustID corpus** — hundreds of gigabytes, 41 days stale, and the same ~117 s ceiling.
- **dejavu / audfprint / Echoprint** — Python+MySQL, unmaintained since 2019, and archived respectively; all would need both a port and a reference database.
- **Qfp** — the only system that measurably works on real DJ mixes (0.741/0.876 accuracy) and its only implementation has no LICENSE file and no commit since 2018.
- **Shazam's `amp.shazam.com` endpoint / ShazamKit** — zero CORS headers from any origin including shazam.com itself, and ShazamKit ships no Web SDK (iOS/macOS/tvOS/watchOS/visionOS + Android only).
- **RapidAPI Shazam wrappers** — CORS is genuinely open and a pasted key would work; rejected for being reverse-engineered private endpoints with no offset semantics and no stability guarantee.
- **Gracenote, Audible Magic, Pex/Vobile, Musiio** — no developer surface at all: a dead portal that refuses port 443, contact-sales only, contact-sales only, and a redirect to soundcloud.com respectively.
- **AHA Music** — no public API by their own FAQ; kept only as the public price oracle for ACRCloud full-file scanning ($1/hr retail).
- **AudD standard endpoint** — no `offset` parameter at all, a 10 MB cap, and their own docs route DJ sets to the enterprise endpoint instead.
- **ACRCloud Identification API** — bytes-only, ≤5 MB, no long-file behaviour; strictly worse than the same vendor's File Scanning API for this job.
- **`soundcloud-setlist-extractor`** — scrapes the `client_id`, resolves the signed HLS stream and fingerprints it; read it for the description-first architecture, do not run it.
- **GitHub Actions as the proxy** — minutes are free on a public repo and the AUP objection is weaker than it first reads (the clause ends "*but a low benefit Action could be ok if it's also low burden*"), but public logs and artifacts plus a manual artifact hand-off make it option C with extra steps.
- **Serato / Mixed In Key / MixesDB-as-a-tool** — Serato logs what *you* played from your own library, Mixed In Key analyses files you already hold, neither identifies someone else's mix.

## 8. Open questions only the owner can answer

1. **Is a draft with holes worth building at all?** No option here returns "every song, in order, with the time it starts". The best free browser path resolved **32.6% of one real 190-minute mix**, and the published academic ceiling with a perfect database is 25–74% per second. If the expectation is the full tracklist, the honest answer is to say so before any code is written.
2. **Would the owner seek AudD's written permission to cache results?** Their recovered terms forbid caching data "in order to re-use it instead of making similar or the same request" — and a tracklist the app cannot persist means re-paying $0.38–$1.50 every time a set is reopened. The clause contemplates permission; nobody has asked. This is the Discogs question again, and it must be settled before anything AudD-derived enters IndexedDB.
3. **Is copying a mix the uploader marked `downloadable: false` acceptable?** User Terms (i) permits copying only "lorsque l'Uploader a consenti à ces pratiques". Options B and C both depend on this; A and D avoid it entirely. A stated risk, not a technical blocker — and only the owner can settle it, once rather than per mix.
4. **Is TrackId.net acceptable as the default path with no terms of use?** Free, keyless, browser-callable, ~384,000 mixes — and no terms-of-use page exists at any probed URL (a privacy policy does, silent on API access). The 2026-09-05 research rejected Discogs outright on terms; overturning that standard here is a decision, not a finding.
5. **Is a second setting, a Cloudflare account and a second deploy target acceptable?** The Worker is ~30 lines and $0/month, but CLAUDE.md's "Only one setting exists: `VITE_SPOTIFY_CLIENT_ID`" stops being true, and option B additionally requires AudD's **paid** plan. The owner has never been asked about a server component or a paid service. Note the answer changes nothing for the *audio* question: the API ToU purpose clauses bind whoever holds the credentials on whatever machine.
6. **Is set prep by a working DJ "commercial"?** YouTube's "usage personnel et non commercial", ACRCloud §13's "internal business purposes", AcoustID's "non-commercial use only" and Deezer's identical framing all turn on this one unanswered question. Answering it once settles several features.
7. **How many of the mixes the owner actually cares about are their own, or their friends'?** If the answer is "most", the master-file and ask-the-uploader routes cover the ground and every blocked option becomes irrelevant.
8. **Would the owner run one measurement before any design work?** Two cheap ones decide most of this: run oEmbed over 30–50 links they would *actually* paste (the 18% figure is query-biased and the unbiased frames read zero), and spend AudD's free 300 requests on a file POST of one mix whose tracklist they already know. Neither needs a design, a payment or an answer to the SoundCloud-fetch question.
9. **Would they rather correct a draft by hand than chase accuracy?** An editable artist/title per row, stored beside the detected value, turns a one-third-complete scan into a usable set document — and it is cheaper to build than any accuracy improvement available here.
10. **Is an off-app desktop step acceptable at all?** Every route that touches audio is shape 2 (laptop, then import, like the Rekordbox XML). If the answer is "phone only", the list collapses to option A plus option D, and nothing else.
