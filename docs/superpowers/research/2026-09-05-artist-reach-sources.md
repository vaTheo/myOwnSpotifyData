# Artist reach ("how well known is this artist") sources for the DJ app (2026-09-05)

Output of a research workflow (5 researchers, 5 fact-checkers with live probes, 1 synthesis). Scope: free sources of an artist-level *reach* number — followers, fans, listeners, DJ support, chart or gig presence — for the artists in the owner's Spotify playlists, usable from a browser-only static Preact app on GitHub Pages (no server, no secrets), underground house/techno first.

Everything below was probed on 2026-09-05 from France with curl and cross-checked by a second reviewer; refuted claims are dropped or corrected in place, unverifiable ones carry **(unverified)**. Six headline claims were re-probed a third time during synthesis (`raw/synthesis/`). Reference material for an "emerging artists" feature; the design spec, once written, is the authority.

Raw probe output is re-readable under `raw/`: `raw/dj-stores/`, `raw/community/`, `raw/streaming-open/`, `raw/open-knowledge/`, `raw/spotify-and-paid/` (first pass), `raw/factcheck-dj-stores/`, `raw/check-community/`, `raw/check-streaming-open/`, `raw/check-open-knowledge/`, `raw/check-spotify-and-paid/` (verification pass), and `raw/synthesis/` (the six headline re-probes run while writing this).

Coverage sample used throughout: 24 artists from very famous to obscure — Peggy Gou, FISHER, Charlotte de Witte, Fred again.., Kerri Chandler, Moodymann, Folamour, Overmono, Skee Mask, Anz, Palms Trax, Avalon Emerson, Jayda G, Shanti Celeste, Cinthie, Kettama, Anetha, Bambounou, Youandewan, Byron the Aquarius, Adryiano, Hugo LX, Baltra, Roza Terenzi.

## 1. The short answer

**What can be had.** Spotify itself no longer tells this app anything about how well known an artist is: for a Development Mode app `GET /artists/{id}` no longer returns `followers` or `popularity` ([developer.spotify.com migration guide](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/web-api/concepts/migration-guide), "The following fields have been removed from response objects… Artist followers, popularity"). Two free replacements survive every gate — both callable from the browser on `https://vatheo.github.io`, needing no key and no secret. Both know all 24 sample artists including the most obscure, but with an important asymmetry: ListenBrainz returned a real number for **24/24** through the exact MBID route the app will use, whereas Deezer's 24/24 is a *name-search* figure and the app is forbidden to search by name (§4.2). Deezer's mandatory ISRC route was probed for 5 of the 24 and measured at 89.7% per ISRC and 12/12 per artist on an obscure-weighted subset — under a looser ISRC rule than §4.2 now requires, so its real coverage is **(unverified)** and is the first thing to measure on the owner's library. **ListenBrainz** gives *total_user_count* — how many ListenBrainz users have ever played that artist (FISHER 5,051; Hugo LX 54), verified today at `https://api.listenbrainz.org/1/stats/artist/886dc0c9-3351-4d2d-b762-060cf1e66929/listeners` with `access-control-allow-origin: *`. **Deezer** gives *nb_fan* — how many Deezer users pressed follow (FISHER 86,639; Hugo LX 585), verified today at `https://api.deezer.com/artist/56125`; Deezer sends **no** `access-control-allow-origin` header at all, so it can only be reached by JSONP (`?output=jsonp&callback=cb`, `content-type: text/javascript`). Both are small, platform-specific audiences, not Spotify monthly listeners: they are honest as *"Deezer fans"* and *"ListenBrainz listeners"* and dishonest as anything else. They also disagree: across all 276 pairs of the 24 artists they order **19.9%** of pairs in opposite directions (Kendall tau-a 0.601), so there is no single true fame ranking to be had here, and no blended "fame score" should be shown.

**What cannot be had, and what to do instead.** The numbers a DJ would actually trust — Resident Advisor followers and upcoming gigs, "played by N DJs" from 1001Tracklists, Beatport chart position, SoundCloud followers — are all unreachable for a static site: RA runs an origin allowlist (`Origin: https://ra.co` gets a header back, `Origin: https://vatheo.github.io` gets none) *and* its terms 4.4(f) forbid automated access; 1001Tracklists has no API; Beatport's CORS is actually fine but there is no anonymous tier at all; SoundCloud needs a paid Artist Pro subscription *and* a client secret ("All clients are currently treated as confidential rather than public, meaning a secret is required to obtain a token" — [developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/guide](https://developers.soundcloud.com/docs/api/guide)). Because no free source ranks the underground tail well, the honest design is **not** a ranking but a filter: use **Wikidata + Wikipedia pageviews** to *subtract* the artists who are demonstrably famous (they are keyed on the Spotify artist id itself, cost one request for the whole library, and are CC0), then show the two audience numbers side by side for everyone who is left, sorted by whichever the owner picks. Matching is the part that quietly breaks everything else — an exact-name search for FISHER returns a 13-fan homonym on Deezer and the actress "India Fisher" on MusicBrainz — so every lookup starts from the Spotify artist id the app already holds, resolved through **MusicBrainz's reverse URL lookup** (`/ws/2/url?resource=https://open.spotify.com/artist/{id}&inc=artist-rels`), which returned the correct artist for 24/24 of the sample and is the single most load-bearing piece of the design.

## 4. Recommended architecture

### 4.1 The shape: subtract the famous, then order what is left

The owner asked for a ranking by reach. The evidence does not support one: no free source orders the underground tail, and the two that cover it disagree on a fifth of all pairs. What the evidence *does* support is a two-stage filter.

1. **Subtract the demonstrably famous.** One Wikidata SPARQL POST over the whole library maps Spotify artist ids to Wikidata items; artists with an item, sitelinks and real pageviews are known quantities and drop out of the "emerging" list. This is exact (no name matching), CC0, and costs one request.
2. **Order what remains by the two audience numbers, side by side, never summed.** ListenBrainz listeners and Deezer fans, each labelled with its own platform, each sortable, with the disagreement visible rather than averaged away.
3. **Weight by the owner's own data.** The app already knows how many of the owner's tracks are by each artist and how often they were played (history import). "Artists you play a lot who almost nobody follows" is a better answer to the actual question than any single external number, and it needs no new source.

### 4.2 Matching strategy — this is the part that breaks

Everything starts from the **Spotify artist id** the app already holds. Name search is disqualified: it returned a 13-fan homonym for FISHER on Deezer, the actress "India Fisher" on MusicBrainz (at score 100), and did not return FISHER's Wikidata item *at all* among 8 results.

- **Spotify artist id → MusicBrainz MBID.** `GET https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/url?resource=https://open.spotify.com/artist/{id}&inc=artist-rels&fmt=json`, take the relation whose `type` is `free streaming`. Present for **24/24** of the sample. Filter relations on `/artist/` — Cinthie's entry also carries an `open.spotify.com/user/…` URL. Cache the MBID **permanently**; MBIDs do not change.
- **MBID → ListenBrainz.** Direct. The response echoes `artist_name`, so verify it against the Spotify name before storing. An unknown MBID returns **HTTP 204 with an empty body** — not 404, not an error object — so parse defensively.
- **Spotify artist id → Wikidata QID.** One SPARQL POST with a `VALUES ?sid { … }` block against `?item wdt:P1902 ?sid`. For artists that miss, retry through MusicBrainz: MBID → `?item wdt:P434 ?mbid`. That chain took the sample from 10/24 to **15/24** and cut false-underground verdicts from 7 to 2. **Never `SAMPLE` on P434 or P1902** — both are multi-valued (1,711 items site-wide carry more than one P1902; Moodymann's item carries two P434 values and only the second one has the Spotify relation), and sampling is exactly what produced a wrong "not recoverable" verdict during research.
- **QID → Wikipedia title → pageviews.** Take the article title from the Wikidata sitelink; never guess one. Sum **en + fr**: Anetha gets more French views (4,331) than English (3,227), so an English-only rule would systematically understate this owner's European artists. Both a 404 and a `200` with `views: 0` are possible for a title with no data, so treat neither status as proof the title is right — verify with `https://en.wikipedia.org/api/rest_v1/page/summary/{title}` when a number looks anomalous. Request only **complete** months; the current month reads as a collapse (Kettama's August-2026 bucket is 402 against a 12,791 monthly average).
- **Artist → Deezer, via a single-artist ISRC.** `GET /track/isrc:{ISRC}` → `artist.id` → `GET /artist/{id}` → `nb_fan`. `track.artist` is the *release's main artist*, so 6 of 61 resolved ISRCs during research landed on a different entity and returned a silently wrong number: Bambounou → "Bambounou x Bruce" **7** (real 9,528), Anetha → Niki Istrefi **1,418** (real 13,984), Hugo LX → Asynchrone **244** (real 585). Contributor name-matching cannot fix it — strict matching rejects the correct "Byron Aquarius", loose matching accepts "Bambounou x Bruce". The rule:
  1. Draw ISRCs **only** from playlist tracks whose Spotify artist array is exactly this one artist.
  2. Normalise and compare the returned Deezer `artist.name` to the Spotify name; on mismatch, try the next candidate ISRC.
  3. If no single-artist ISRC exists for that artist, **show nothing** and mark it unresolved. Never show a number from a collaboration or a remix credit.
  This matters in the harmful direction: a spuriously small `nb_fan` marks an established artist as underground, which is precisely the answer the feature exists to give.
- **Verification oracle.** `https://open.spotify.com/oembed?url=https://open.spotify.com/artist/{id}` returns Spotify's own canonical artist name with `access-control-allow-origin: *` and is explicitly permitted by `open.spotify.com/robots.txt`. Use it whenever a display name has to be compared across services.

### 4.3 Quotas and job design (planning figure: 1,000 distinct artists)

Same shape as the existing history import and playlist sync — **manual, resumable, checkpointed, never on page load**.

| Step | Calls | Pace | Wall clock for 1,000 artists |
|---|---|---|---|
| MusicBrainz MBID resolution | 1 per artist (cached forever) | 1 req/s documented | ~17 min, once |
| ListenBrainz listeners | 1 per artist | 1 req/s documented; live window is 30 per 10 s and **distinct MBIDs are cache misses that each decrement it** | ~17 min |
| Deezer fans | 2 per artist (ISRC + artist), JSONP | ≤8 req/s (quota ~50 per 5 s per IP) | ~4 min |
| Wikidata SPARQL | 1 POST per ~200-id batch | one shot | seconds **(unverified at 1,000 ids — 3 ids took 1.05 s; chunk to stay inside the 60 s timeout)** |
| Wikipedia pageviews | 2 per artist **that has an article** (en + fr) | undocumented; sequential is fine | ~2–3 min |

- **Failure handling.** MusicBrainz 503 means *rate-limited or globally busy*, **never "artist absent"** — three independent triggers are documented, so a longer sleep is not a fix. Retry with exponential backoff and record a distinct `unknown, retry later` state. Deezer quota errors arrive as **HTTP 200** with `error.code: 4` in the body, so inspect the body, not the status; and JSONP has no status codes at all, so impose a per-request timeout in the app and treat a silent timeout as a retry, not a miss.
- **Cache (IndexedDB), mirroring the BPM design.** `artistIdentity` keyed by Spotify artist id (`{ mbid, qid, deezerArtistId, matchedBy, verifiedName, resolvedAt }`, permanent). `artistReach` keyed by `spotifyArtistId + source` (`{ value, unit, fetchedAt, sourceUrl }`) with a long TTL — these numbers move slowly; a 90-day refresh is plenty. `artistLookupState` keyed by `spotifyArtistId + source` recording `notFound` / `unknownRetryLater` with a retry date so a failed pass is not re-run blindly. Export all three inside the existing zip so a laptop run reaches the phone.
- **Everything is per-artist idempotent**, so the job can be stopped and resumed at any point, and a partial result is still useful — the list simply shows fewer resolved artists.

### 4.4 What to show the DJ

- **Two numbers, two labels, no arithmetic between them.** `1 012 ListenBrainz listeners · 703 Deezer fans` — never "followers", never "monthly listeners", never a combined "fame score" or a 0–100 index. The 19.9% pair disagreement makes any blend a fiction, and the labels are the only thing keeping the numbers honest, since neither platform's audience is Spotify's.
- **A "public profile" line rather than a third number**: `Wikipedia article · 19 languages · 289k views/yr` for the famous, `no Wikipedia article` for the rest. That is the filter, stated plainly.
- **"No data" is not zero.** An artist the app could not resolve must read *no reach data* with a retry affordance, because treating absence as zero would rank a resolution failure identically to a genuinely tiny artist — the exact wrong answer.
- **Own-data columns beside the external ones**: tracks in the owner's playlists, plays from the imported history. The sort that answers the owner's actual question is "artists I play a lot, with the smallest audience", and both halves of that are already local.
- **Provenance and freshness on every number**: source name, `as of <date>`, and a link to the artist's page on that service. Wikipedia-derived text and figures carry CC BY-SA attribution.
- **Where it lives.** This is a Crate-family surface (the crate-digging view), gated on the artist-reach job having run at least once, reached from Settings like the other manual jobs — Import is the precedent: a route that is not a tab.

### 4.5 Terms risk

- **Deezer** is the sharpest edge. Its terms *expressly* contemplate this use — "develop personal web pages, blog… and personal applications" — but §IV limits it to "a non-commercial purpose and in a non-commercial environment" and enumerates that the developer "shall not perceive, receive, generate, benefit or create directly or indirectly, any moneys, incomes, revenues, **data** or any other consideration". A working DJ preparing paid sets is arguably commercial; the word "data" sits awkwardly next to building a derived reach ranking, even read in context as a consideration clause. The useful negative: **no clause restricts caching or local storage**, so the IndexedDB design is clear. §II(a) contemplates a registration step even though the endpoints are keyless on the wire, and §II(c) reserves the right to withdraw access "without any prior notice" — JSONP has been toggled before.
- **MusicBrainz** asks for a self-identifying User-Agent that browser JavaScript cannot set (`User-Agent` is a forbidden header). The same gap was accepted in the BPM research and tolerated in practice; `Api-User-Agent` returns 200 but is undocumented at MusicBrainz **(unverified whether it is acceptable to them)**. Stay at 1 req/s and back off on 503.
- **Wikidata is CC0** (no attribution required) and **Wikipedia pageviews content is CC BY-SA 4.0** (attribution and share-alike) — credit both.
- **ListenBrainz** is keyless and open; its redistribution licence was not read **(unverified)** and is very likely moot, since numbers are shown only to the person who fetched them. Its documented *popularity* endpoints already moved behind auth during research, so the one working stats endpoint could close the same way — degrade gracefully.
- **Spotify exposure** is lower here than in the BPM work: the app sends artist ids (not Spotify Content) to MusicBrainz and Wikidata, and stores only third-party numbers about the owner's own library. Keep to that tier — no library-wide harvesting, nothing published.

## 5. Rejected sources, one line each

- **Spotify Web API `GET /artists/{id}`** — `followers` and `popularity` removed for Development Mode apps; Extended Quota Mode needs an organisation and 250k MAU.
- **Spotify oEmbed as a reach source** — carries no audience metric at all (kept in §4.2 as a name-verification utility).
- **open.spotify.com internal / pathfinder GraphQL** — undocumented internal endpoints, against Spotify's terms, and they die without notice; deliberately not probed.
- **charts.spotify.com** — JS-only SPA shell, CSV behind a login, no CORS header, and top-200-shaped so blank for the whole underground tail.
- **kworb.net** — per-artist pages are keyed on the Spotify artist id, graded (11/24, Fred again.. 5.6bn streams), and fresher than any dataset, but **no `access-control-allow-origin` header on any response**; coverage also stops above roughly 60k followers.
- **Beatport** — CORS is fine (`*`, `authorization` allowed in preflight) but every catalog endpoint and the OpenAPI schema return 401 and there is no anonymous tier; "Hype" is a $9.99/month subscription gated to labels under $25k of sales, i.e. label-level, opt-in and binary, not an artist reach measure.
- **Traxsource** — Cloudflare managed challenge (403) to server-side curl, and no CORS header for a browser to use.
- **JunoDownload** — the digital store shut down; every URL 301s to a goodbye page. (Juno Records, `juno.co.uk`, is alive but Cloudflare-403.)
- **Bandcamp** — `robots.txt` disallows `/api/` outright, and the JSON endpoint carrying fan counts sends no CORS header; two independent failures.
- **Boomkat** — Cloudflare 403, and no artist-level numeric of any kind exists to rank by.
- **Hardwax** — plain HTML answers curl, but no CORS header and no artist metric in the page.
- **Resident Advisor** — conceptually the perfect source (`followerCount` + `upcomingEventsCount`), blocked by an origin allowlist *and* independently by terms 4.4(f) barring automated access; a human looking artists up by hand is of course still fine.
- **1001Tracklists** — "played by N DJs" is the truest DJ-support metric in existence and there is no API: `api.1001tracklists.com` presents a self-signed certificate, artist pages carry no CORS header, `robots.txt` sets `Crawl-delay: 8`.
- **Discogs** — browser-callable and free, but the API Terms (Wayback snapshot 2026-05-30; the live page 403s to every User-Agent) forbid displaying Content "more than six (6) hours older" than Discogs' own and forbid caching it beyond immediate need, which rules out this app's persisted-then-browsed pattern; the metric is also a weak, unstable proxy for reach (ρ +0.03 to +0.35, n≈17) and would need per-datum attribution plus a non-`nofollow` link on every row.
- **SoundCloud** — needs a `client_secret` a static site cannot hold ("All clients are currently treated as confidential"), on top of a paid Artist Pro subscription to register at all.
- **Mixcloud** — best transport in the study (CORS echo + JSONP + no auth) and the wrong population: MusicBrainz carries a Mixcloud link for 1/24, search top hits are wrong for 11–16 of 24, and most electronic producers never maintain an account.
- **Songkick** — "We are currently not approving API requests for student projects, educational purposes or hobbyist purposes", plus a licence fee.
- **Bandsintown** — no `access-control-allow-origin` on any response, so a valid `app_id` would not help; MusicBrainz link coverage 2/24.
- **Setlist.fm** — API key requires registration (not attempted); setlist documentation is far sparser for club DJs than for touring bands **(unverified — coverage unmeasured)**.
- **Last.fm** — probably the strongest signal available, blocked by four obstacles: ToS 2.7 requires prior written approval for public pages plus a "powered by AudioScrobbler" button and deep links, a 100 MB usage cap with a caching obligation, and clause 4.4 rate limiting. Coverage on the 24 is **(unverified)**. Owner's call, not a research verdict — see §6.
- **YouTube Data API v3** — no path from a Spotify artist id: Wikidata's P2397 covers 4/24 and all four are the *most famous*, zero underground; subscriber counts are also "rounded down to three significant figures" and can be hidden entirely.
- **Genius** — the `Authorization` preflight returns HTTP 403 so a browser refuses the request, a valid token would have to be baked into a public repo, and `followers_count` measures lyrics-site engagement, close to noise for instrumental house and techno.
- **Apple Music** — needs a developer-token JWT signed with a private key from a paid membership; a server-side secret by construction.
- **Tidal OpenAPI** — needs OAuth client credentials including a secret.
- **Chartmetric** — "Treat refresh tokens like passwords. Never commit them or expose them in client-side code", and the browser is blocked at CORS before auth is even reached.
- **Soundcharts** — "Keep your client secret private. Do not expose it in frontend code, public repositories, logs, shared screenshots, or **browser-based applications**" ([developers.soundcharts.com/api/authorization](https://developers.soundcharts.com/api/authorization)); the free trial is 1,000 requests total.
- **Songstats** — the preflight pins `access-control-allow-origin` to `https://developers.stats.company`, so no other site can call it with any key.
- **Viberate / Spotontrack / Musicstax** — no public API surface (404s from nginx), NXDOMAIN, and a Cloudflare bot challenge respectively.
- **Cyanite** — paid, and it tags audio rather than measuring reach.
- **HuggingFace `flaser381/spotify-avd`** — technically the best result in the whole study (2.2M artists keyed by Spotify artist id, 24/24 on the sample correctly graded, `access-control-allow-origin` verified from our exact origin) but it is derived from the same Spotify scrape corpus the BPM research already ruled "do not use or re-host"; also a 2025-07 snapshot biased low for exactly the fast-growing artists the feature is for, a `cc-by-nc-4.0` tag the uploader cannot grant over Spotify's data, an undisclosed popularity ≥ 5 floor, 26 downloads and 1 like, and 104 MB of parquet to pull into a phone browser. Recorded in full so nobody re-researches it; see §6.
- **HuggingFace `agusbegue/spotify-artists`** — artist *name* only with no Spotify id, 2021 data, and an Argentinian chart slice rather than a catalogue.
- **Kaggle `sarahjeffreson/large-random-spotify-artist-sample-with-metadata`** — better shaped than any other dataset (ids, followers, popularity and real `monthly_listeners`) and genuinely browser-fetchable through Kaggle's stable download endpoint, but it is a 37,000-row *random sample* of Spotify's millions: **4/24** on the sample. Worth keeping as a one-off oracle for checking other sources' follower values.

## 6. Open questions only the owner can answer

1. **Is a Spotify-scrape-derived dataset acceptable, or does the BPM research precedent stand?** `docs/superpowers/research/2026-09-05-bpm-key-sources.md` already decided "do not use or re-host" for this corpus. `flaser381/spotify-avd` is the only source found that returns a graded Spotify follower number for the underground tail, matched exactly by Spotify artist id, and it is browser-fetchable. Overturning that precedent is a decision, not a research finding — and it would still be a 14-month-old snapshot.
2. **Is Deezer's non-commercial framing acceptable for a working DJ?** Their terms name "personal applications" as the intended case, then restrict use to a "non-commercial purpose and in a non-commercial environment". Preparing paid sets is arguably commercial. This is the same unresolved question the BPM research raised for Deezer previews; answering it once settles both features.
3. **Register a Last.fm API key?** It is very likely the best single signal in this study (`listeners` + `playcount` over a large, electronic-friendly user base) and its coverage on the 24 is completely unmeasured. But ToS 2.7 requires *prior written approval in writing* before a public page uses it, plus a visible "powered by AudioScrobbler" button and deep links back. Only the owner can register the key and decide whether to seek that approval.
4. **Would the owner knowingly deviate from Discogs' terms, or drop Discogs?** Compliant use means fetching live on every view (~16 minutes at 25 req/min before a 200-artist list renders) and attributing every datum with a linked "Data provided by Discogs." The alternative is to drop it — which also drops nothing the owner asked for, since it measures vinyl collecting rather than reach.
5. **Two numbers or one?** The recommendation is to show ListenBrainz listeners and Deezer fans side by side and never blend them. If the owner wants a single sort order, they must pick which platform's audience they trust for their scene, knowing the two disagree on a fifth of all pairs.
6. **What does the real library look like?** Everything here is measured on a deliberately underground-weighted sample of 24. The Wikidata resolution rate (15/24), **how many artists actually have a track credited to them alone** (which is what decides whether Deezer is a primary source or a partial one), and the number of distinct artists across 50–200 playlists are all one experiment away once the job can read the library — and they decide whether this feature is worth its ~40 minutes of lookups.
7. **Does the owner already have an RA, Beatport or SoundCloud account they would rather read by hand?** The three best metrics in this study are all reachable to a human in a browser and to no static web app. A "paste the number" affordance, or a manual note per artist, is a legitimate design and costs nothing in terms risk.
